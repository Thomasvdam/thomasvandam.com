# Thomas van Dam and Llamateur: a shared visual family

Thomasvdam.com is the home of the person and his projects. Llamateur is an
independent space for writing and learning in public. A visitor should be able
to recognize the same point of view across both sites without mistaking them
for the same publication.

This guide describes the relationship. It is not a component specification:
each site keeps its own repository, layout, and visual decisions.

## Shared cues

- **Editorial hierarchy.** Lead with a clear, expressive headline. Use small,
  precise labels for context such as section names, dates, or edition notes.
- **Visible navigation.** Make links look and read like invitations to explore.
  Arrows can signal movement to another page or project when they add meaning;
  their exact treatment belongs to each site.
- **Human voice.** Write with curiosity and a little humor. Favor specific,
  plain language over polished brand slogans. Let work in progress feel like
  work in progress.
- **Deliberate imperfection.** A small offset, rotation, annotation, or unusual
  composition can make a page feel handmade. Keep reading and navigation easy.
- **Clear authorship.** When the sites refer to each other, name the
  relationship directly: Llamateur is a writing project by Thomas van Dam.
  Link to the other site where that context helps the reader.

These are family traits, not a checklist to apply to every page. Repeated
choices should feel intentional rather than mechanically identical.

## Each site's identity

| | Thomasvdam.com | Llamateur |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Role | Personal home, work, and experiments | Writing and learning notebook |
| Mood | Dark, energetic, tactile | Light, curious, editorial |
| Color | Dark ground, warm paper, orange emphasis | Paper and ink, with lime and coral accents |
| Type and composition | Condensed display type, broad gestures, visual experiments | Publication-like structure, annotations, and room to read |
| Primary name | Thomas van Dam | Llamateur |

Keep each site's name, logo, palette, and page structure distinct. Shared cues
should support recognition without making either site look like a reskin of the
other.

## Using the guide

For a new page or visual change, ask:

1. Does it feel at home on this site first?
2. Is at least one shared cue visible where it naturally fits?
3. Would someone moving between the sites understand that they have the same
   author, especially at the point of a cross-site link?

Review both sites side by side when their designs change substantially. Update
this guide when the relationship itself changes; do not synchronize every color,
component, or spacing value merely to keep the sites similar.
